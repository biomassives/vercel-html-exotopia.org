## Lamu-Based Community Platform Launches Cryptographic Privacy Shield for Coastal Field Workers — and Pays Them in KES

**New open-source system uses advanced mathematics to verify environmental work offline, trigger M-Pesa payments, and protect the identity of field workers without storing personal data on any server**

---

**NAIROBI / LAMU COUNTY, Kenya — June 30, 2026** — SCD Hub, the sustainable community development hub operating through its global platform Exotopia.org, has announced the launch of a new zero-knowledge proof system designed specifically for the conditions facing environmental field workers in coastal Kenya: intermittent 3G connectivity, the need to protect contributor identities in politically sensitive monitoring work, and the requirement to trigger KES payments via M-Pesa without routing personal financial information through a centralised server.

The system, built on a branch of mathematics called the E8 root lattice — an eight-dimensional geometric structure used in physics to describe fundamental symmetries — generates a cryptographic proof of a field worker's activity entirely on their mobile device, without an internet connection. The proof confirms that a verified contributor completed specific environmental work (coastal plastic collection, water quality monitoring, ecosystem survey) and embeds the material metric — kilograms collected, litres tested, species documented — directly into the mathematical proof. When connectivity returns, the proof is verified in under one second at the network edge, a verified payment trigger is sent to the M-Pesa B2C channel, and the platform anchor is written to the Algorand blockchain.

At no point does the system store the worker's phone number, precise GPS coordinates, or financial balance in any database accessible to a third party.

"The communities we work with in Lamu County have very legitimate reasons not to trust that their data is safe," said Greg Willson, founder of SCD Hub. "A field monitor documenting illegal waste dumping near a corporate operation is not in the same situation as someone posting to Instagram. The proof system we have built is designed for that reality. The mathematics proves the work happened. It does not prove who did it."

The field operation is anchored in Mpeketoni, Lamu County, where Muirithi Jariffe leads the local community group using SCD Hub's Eco-Ledger system — a table banking-style accounting tool adapted for ecological contributions. The Eco-Ledger allows members to record species sightings, environmental actions, lesson completions, and knowledge shared at each group meeting, building a community-held record that accumulates across seasons and can be used to support grant applications, school partnerships, and conservation authority reporting.

The new proof system extends the Eco-Ledger into the cryptographic layer: each contribution is now mathematically verifiable by any third party — a NEMA inspector, an NGO funder, a county government authority — without the verifying party needing to access any personally identifiable information.

The platform also launches three vocational learning tracks specifically designed for communities working without reliable internet access:

- **WATSAN Track** — Water, Sanitation and Hygiene, covering SODIS solar water disinfection, biosand filter construction, CLTS community sanitation facilitation, and water quality testing with field instruments
- **Solar Track** — Solar thermal cooking, biomass cookstove efficiency, and photovoltaic system sizing for off-grid households
- **Citizen Science Track** — iNaturalist species recording, freshwater macroinvertebrate BMWP scoring, transect surveys, and phenological monitoring

Each track issues internationally recognised digital credentials aligned with the W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 standard and Open Badges 3.0, making them importable into professional and educational platforms globally. Certificates are co-signed by the community group and the relevant partner organisation, with the full learning evidence stored on IPFS and verifiable without a smartphone.

The platform is free to use. All code is open-source under GPL v3 and can be run by any organisation that wants to deploy their own instance.
