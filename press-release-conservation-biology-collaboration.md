# SCD Hub Seeks Conservation Biology Partners to Co-Author Pollinator Habitat Network Guidance for Exotopia.org

**[CITY, STATE] — [DATE, 2026]** — SCD Hub, the organization behind the citizen-science and habitat-restoration programs on Exotopia.org, today issued an open call to conservation biologists, restoration ecologists, and pollinator-network organizations to help design and vet a new pollinator stepping-stone planting guidance system — and to review the field protocols behind its existing citizen-science monitoring programs.

## The ask, plainly

This is a request to **co-author**, not a request to test something already finished. The planting-guidance system — small, expert-designed native plantings sited close enough together that short-flight-range pollinators (many native solitary bees forage only a few hundred meters) can move through fragmented urban and suburban land — is specified but **not yet built**. Regional Pollinator Pathway networks already do exactly this kind of siting in the field; what we're building is software to distribute expert-vetted regional plans, help residents and towns get legal access to marginal land (road edges, parking-lot islands, utility margins), and keep plantings maintained over time.

We deliberately did not build this as a crowd-sourced or self-authored system. Planting guidance needs to be right before it reaches a homeowner or a town administrator — closer to a vetted field manual than a wiki. That means it needs partner organizations willing to author or review regional species lists, siting criteria, and maintenance calendars directly, attributed by name. Right now our content cites existing regional pollinator-pathway authorities; we're asking whether your organization would go further and author directly.

## What already exists, for context

We're not asking anyone to build credibility for us from zero. Real, shipped programs already running through the same platform:

- **PFAS/PFOA decontamination tracking** — citizen-logged field progress against a vetted remediation methods library.
- **Tick Watch Northeast** — a tick-ecology and Lyme-risk education/mapping program for secondary schools, community groups, and trail crews, with an open call for entomologists and public-health clinicians.
- **Cyanobacteria Watch** — harmful-algal-bloom and lake-eutrophication monitoring, seeking limnologists and state HAB program staff.
- **Freshwater Phenology Network** — a national stream-health monitoring thread connecting the regional programs above.
- An **eco-ops field library** cross-linked to hub.approvideo.org's appropriate-technology video collection, and a mentor/reward-points system that already supports subject-matter experts reviewing and confirming community members' field work.

None of this is invented for this letter — it's the same infrastructure the pollinator-guidance work would sit alongside, and the same "Knowledge Contributor" recognition already extended to SME contributors on the programs above would extend to planting-plan co-authors.

## What we're specifically asking conservation biologists for

- **Co-author or review** a regional pollinator-network planting plan: target species list (native, guild-appropriate, favoring short-flight-range foragers), siting criteria for marginal land, and a maintenance calendar — attributed to you or your organization.
- **Protocol review** for existing field programs (tick ecology, cyanobacteria/HAB monitoring, freshwater macroinvertebrate/BMWP calibration) where your specialty applies.
- **A conversation**, if you're not ready to commit content yet, about what a real partnership would need to look like — data-sharing terms, attribution, liability, or anything else your institution requires before putting a name on public guidance.

## What we are not asking for

Money, exclusivity, or your organization's brand on anything before you've reviewed it. Nothing publishes under your name without your sign-off. This platform does not sell data, does not require a blockchain wallet or purchase of any kind to participate, and keeps participant data on-device by default.
