# One Thing First

**Why we rebuilt the Exotopia onboarding around a single committed first step — and what the five paths are**

*July 2026 · Community · Field*

---

The old Exotopia onboarding asked you to pick a role (field worker, artist, educator, coordinator, explorer) and a community group before you had any reason to care about either. By step two you were choosing between Funky Pony and Fana Ka without knowing what either did in the context of the platform. The flow was fast but it wasn't deliberate. People skipped through it.

The new onboarding is slower on purpose.

---

## The Principle

Get one commitment before anything else. Not a role. Not a community affiliation. A specific, named first action — something a person can actually do in the next ten minutes or the next week.

This is the design constraint: *you cannot reserve your settlement until you have chosen exactly one thing to do first.*

The planet is still there. The cosmic visualization is still there. But the connection between your real-world activity and your settlement address is now the explicit center of the experience, not a footnote at the end.

---

## The Five Paths

### Learning & Discovery

For people who want to understand before they act. The Eco Library draws from ApproVideo and field partners across twelve domains of sustainable living: water, soil, food, energy, biodiversity, climate, restoration, and more. First commitment options:

- Browse the Eco Library — no quiz, follow what interests you
- Take a knowledge quiz on water systems, PFAS basics, soil ecology, or renewable energy
- Read the working notes — our blog covers upstream PFAS producer responsibility, East Coast citizen science networks, library ecology proposals

Settlement growth: a library dome linked to completed reading and modules; knowledge certificates displayed in the dome interior; ecocity infrastructure added as each domain is completed.

### Make a Project

For people who have something they want to build. Projects on Exotopia connect to real places and real communities. First commitment options:

- Start an eco field project — name a site to monitor, log it, start building a field record
- Contribute a resource to the library — a video, guide, or document; a technique, a local species, a traditional practice
- Improve the platform — submit a feature idea, flag something broken, improve documentation

Settlement growth: project beacon objects for active field projects; site pins on the settlement map for monitored locations; contributor credits for accepted platform improvements.

### Train & Certify

For people who want credentials with real-world weight. Each track draws from the ApproVideo library and SCD Hub field partners. Modules run 20–60 minutes. Completing one earns a certificate and an ecocity object. First commitment options:

- Water & Sanitation (WATSAN) — biosand filtration, rainwater harvesting, water quality testing, community water system design
- Citizen Science track — PFAS monitoring basics, freshwater phenology, tick ecology, cyanobacteria identification, community data submission
- Renewable Energy basics — solar sizing, battery systems, micro-hydro, community energy planning

Settlement growth: certificate badges displayed inside the dome; ecocity infrastructure matching the certification domain; educator role unlocked on completing a full track.

### Help Build This Platform

For people with technical or documentation skills who want to contribute directly. Exotopia is open source and actively developed. Every accepted contribution earns settlement credits. First commitment options:

- Find and report an issue — explore the platform and report anything broken, confusing, or missing
- Submit a feature request — we have a structured bounty system for proposals that get built
- Add or improve content — eco library contributions, glossary edits, blog commentary, translations

Settlement growth: contribution credit objects for each accepted issue or feature; builder badge on the settlement profile; bounty earnings convertible to settlement upgrade tokens.

### Eco Field Work

For people already doing environmental monitoring, restoration, or advocacy. This path leads directly to the field data system. The specific framing is intentional: PFOS, PFOA, and thousands of related "forever chemicals" — released by semiconductor manufacturing, firefighting foam, food packaging, and textile production — are active contaminants in 2026 watersheds globally. Citizen science tracking makes them visible and attributable. First commitment options:

- Log a water monitoring site — any stream, pond, tap, wetland, or coastline near you; takes two minutes; add test data and photos over time
- Map a potential PFAS source — document an industrial site, airport, fire station, semiconductor plant, or manufacturing facility near you
- Learn PFAS monitoring basics — how forever chemicals move through watersheds, what field sampling looks like, how to read public contamination databases

Settlement growth: water monitoring station objects for each logged site; field records linked to the settlement and visible to the network; ecosystem restoration objects earned as field data accumulates.

## The Cosmic Layer

The mission framing on step zero of the new onboarding says this explicitly:

> The same living-systems knowledge that applies to a habitat orbiting a black hole applies to the atmosphere, watersheds, and soil of the planet you're standing on right now. That's not metaphor — it's the design.

Ecocity systems — biosand filters, rainwater harvesters, food forests, rain gardens, soil amendment techniques, passive solar design — are documented in the Eco Library because they are needed here, in 2026, on this planet. They are also the design vocabulary for any hypothetical future settlement on a distant world. The cosmic visualization is not an escape. It is a frame that makes the scale of the problem and the scale of the community's response legible at the same time.

## What the Settlement Holds

Each path populates the settlement with specific objects as activity accumulates:

| Path | Settlement objects earned |
|---|---|
| Learning | Library dome, knowledge certificates, domain infrastructure |
| Project | Project beacons, site map pins, contributor credits |
| Certify | Certificate badges, certification-domain ecocity objects, educator role |
| Platform help | Contribution credits, builder badge, bounty tokens |
| Eco field | Monitoring stations, field record links, restoration objects |

The settlement is not a reward for completing onboarding. It is a persistent record of activity that grows in direct proportion to real-world contribution. The Letters Patent deed format (covered in the companion post "Forty Acres and a Mule in the Cosmos") is the on-chain version of that record.

---

## What Is Not in the New Onboarding

The community group selection (Funky Pony, Fana Ka, Uni-Kibaoni, OT Kulcha) is no longer part of the onboarding flow. Those communities are real and their coordination infrastructure is active — but choosing a community affiliation before you know what you're committing to was premature friction. Community discovery is now available through the platform after the first commitment is made.

The participation mode (adult individual / group member / youth participant) is also removed from the flow. This is recoverable context for coordinators and educators, not a gate for first-time visitors.
