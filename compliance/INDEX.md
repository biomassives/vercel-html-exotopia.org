## What we are

Before any jurisdiction analysis, we need to be clear about what Exotopia and SCD Hub provide. Classification varies by country and the answer to "what is this?" determines which regulations apply.

**We provide:**
1. A free-to-use web visualization of astronomical data (public domain / open science data)
2. A permanent address system for digital locations, represented as on-chain records (NFTs on public blockchains)
3. Community tools — event coordination, eco-ops field tracking, vocational education outcomes
4. Optional premium access funded by address sales and network fees

**We do not provide:**
- Financial instruments or investment products (no promised return, no revenue-sharing tokens)
- Currency exchange or money transmission services
- Insurance or custody of third-party assets
- Health, legal, or financial advice

The primary regulatory question in most jurisdictions is whether our on-chain address records (NFTs) are classified as:
- **Digital collectibles / property** — lightest regulation, most favourable
- **Virtual goods / in-game assets** — consumer protection law applies, generally manageable
- **Utility tokens** — varying treatment; often requires registration or disclosure
- **Securities** — heaviest regulation, requires licensing, most disruptive to our model

Our NFTs have no promised return, no equity interest, no revenue participation, and no utility beyond access to a specific place in our system. We believe they fall clearly in the collectibles/property category in most jurisdictions. This belief needs jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction legal verification.

## Our operating strategy

We are an **open-source, community-operated service** built primarily for communities in the Global South — East Africa, Southeast Asia, LATAM — where our partner network (M-Pesa, community facilitators, eco-ops programs) is active. Our strategy for legal existence across jurisdictions is:

1. **Lead with the free tier.** The visualization and browsing are free, open, and contain no financial product. This is clearly protected speech and educational access in virtually every jurisdiction.

2. **Be a tool, not a custodian.** We do not hold user funds. We do not operate a wallet. We do not take custody of user NFTs. Users interact directly with public blockchains via their own keys. We are infrastructure, not a custodian.

3. **Partner locally for payments.** Where direct crypto transactions face regulatory barriers, local partners (M-Pesa integration via pon.ink, Stripe, etc.) handle fiat on-ramps. These partners carry their own appropriate licenses in their jurisdictions.

4. **Operate transparently.** All contracts are open-source and auditable. All fees are disclosed. All on-chain records are public. Regulatory agencies in any jurisdiction can verify what we do.

5. **Prioritise access over compliance arbitrage.** We will not use regulatory differences between countries to extract value from communities with weaker consumer protection. We apply our best standard everywhere, regardless of local requirements.

## Jurisdiction summaries

### 🇺🇸 United States

**Classification risk:** Medium-high  
**Primary concern:** SEC Howey test for whether NFTs are securities; state-level MSB licensing  
**Strategy:** Clear non-investment framing; no secondary market facilitation by us; self-hosted NFT claims  
**Status:** Research needed — see [us/overview.md](us/overview.md) *(pending)*

Key questions:
- Does our NFT structure pass Howey? (No investment of money in a common enterprise with expectation of profit from others' efforts — we believe yes, passes.)
- Do we need to register as a money service business in any state for the network fee collection?
- State-by-state analysis of digital asset property laws (Wyoming, Colorado are most favourable)
- FTC consumer protection requirements for virtual goods disclosure

### 🇪🇺 European Union / EEA

**Classification risk:** Medium (MiCA creates clarity)  
**Primary concern:** MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, applies from Dec 2024), GDPR, DSA  
**Strategy:** Register as unique NFT issuer under MiCA exemption; GDPR-by-design architecture  
**Status:** Research needed — see [eu/overview.md](eu/overview.md) *(pending)*

Key questions:
- MiCA Article 4(2) exemption for unique, non-fungible tokens — do our addresses qualify? Almost certainly yes if each is individually issued and non-interchangeable.
- GDPR data minimisation: we store minimal PII; address metadata is on public chain. Does posting to a public blockchain constitute processing of personal data?
- Digital Services Act: does our user count trigger DSA obligations? (Unlikely at current scale, but monitor.)
- Consumer Rights Directive: cooling-off period for digital content purchases — applies to NFT sales? Precedent mixed.

### 🇬🇧 United Kingdom

**Classification risk:** Medium  
**Primary concern:** FCA crypto asset registration; Consumer Protection regs  
**Strategy:** Qualified as crypto-asset for promotion rules; clear risk disclosures  
**Status:** Research needed — see [uk/overview.md](uk/overview.md) *(pending)*

Key questions:
- UK Financial Promotion Order: do our NFTs fall under the regulated activity definition? Likely no if no financial return promised.
- FCA crypto asset "qualifying cryptoasset" — do we need to register the asset or just the promotion?
- Consumer Rights Act 2015 applicability to digital goods.

### 🌍 East Africa — Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Ethiopia

**Classification risk:** Low-medium (regulatory frameworks developing)  
**Primary concern:** Mobile money integration (M-Pesa / Safaricom regulation); data localisation  
**Strategy:** Primary onboarding via earn-not-pay (no direct crypto purchase needed); partner through pon.ink / M-Pesa-licensed entities  
**Status:** Research needed — see [east-africa/overview.md](east-africa/overview.md) *(pending)*

Key questions:
- Kenya Capital Markets Authority (CMA) position on digital collectibles (2023 policy framework — digital asset framework under development)
- Tanzania: Bank of Tanzania has cautioned against crypto — how does "earning" an address (no cash transaction) interact with this?
- Ethiopia: new digital economy law (2023) — scope and applicability to international services
- M-Pesa regulation: Safaricom operates under CBK (Central Bank of Kenya) — fiat on-ramps via pon.ink are regulated through pon.ink, not us
- Data residency requirements for user data generated during eco-ops field work

### 🌏 Southeast Asia — Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Philippines, Indonesia, Vietnam

**Classification risk:** Varies significantly by country  
**Primary concern:** Indonesia (OJK crypto restrictions), Vietnam (NFT regulation unclear), Thailand SEC  
**Strategy:** Use Singapore as the regional hub entity; localise payment partners by country  
**Status:** Research needed — see [southeast-asia/overview.md](southeast-asia/overview.md) *(pending)*

Key questions:
- Singapore MAS: digital payment token service exemption — does our NFT model qualify for the payment services exemption?
- Indonesia: OJK Regulation 4/2023 and Bappebti digital asset regulations — are collectible NFTs covered? Indonesia has restricted many crypto activities.
- Philippines SEC: "digital financial assets" circular — collectible NFTs appear to be excluded from the framework, but confirmation needed.
- Malaysia SC: digital asset exchange registration — we are not an exchange; collectible issuers appear to have no registration obligation.
- Vietnam: no NFT-specific regulation as of 2024; general digital content laws apply; monitor closely.

### 🌎 Latin America — Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Argentina

**Classification risk:** Medium (Brazil has clear framework; others developing)  
**Primary concern:** Brazil's Law 14,478/2022 (Virtual Asset Services Providers law) and Banco Central regulations  
**Strategy:** Comply with Brazil's VASP framework via pon.ink licensing; treat other LATAM as digital goods  
**Status:** Research needed — see [latam/overview.md](latam/overview.md) *(pending)*

Key questions:
- Brazil: Banco Central VASP registration — are NFT issuers covered? Framework focuses on exchange and custody, not issuance of unique collectibles.
- Mexico CNBV: digital asset regulations — similar to Brazil, focuses on exchange activity.
- Colombia SFC: no NFT-specific law; general consumer protection applies.
- Argentina: heavy capital controls and crypto restrictions — fiat on-ramp options very limited; earn-not-pay model preferred.

### 🇮🇳 South Asia — India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka

**Classification risk:** High (India's tax treatment; Pakistan's ambiguous stance)  
**Primary concern:** India 30% flat tax on digital asset profits, 1% TDS on transfers; unclear classification  
**Strategy:** Earn-not-pay model avoids most direct purchase friction; clearly frame as cultural/educational tool  
**Status:** Research needed — see [south-asia/overview.md](south-asia/overview.md) *(pending)*

Key questions:
- India Income Tax Act Section 115BBH: 30% tax on "virtual digital assets" — does this apply to settlement addresses when earned (not purchased)? Is there a distinction for earning vs buying?
- RBI circular history on crypto: RBI removed its 2018 banking ban in 2020. Banks can service crypto. But regulatory clarity remains incomplete.
- India's proposed "Digital India Act" — monitor for new digital property classification framework.
- Pakistan: SBP and SECP have issued warnings but no binding prohibition on possession of digital collectibles.

### 🇿🇼 Zimbabwe

**Classification risk:** Split — low for the static eco-library node, unassessed for the main platform  
**Primary concern:** Cyber and Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07] (CDPA) and its licensing regulations (SI 155), enforced by POTRAZ (Postal and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe)  
**Strategy:** Treat this as two separate questions rather than one — see below  
**Status:** Research needed — see [zimbabwe/overview.md](zimbabwe/overview.md) *(pending)*

This jurisdiction surfaced a strategy note arguing Exotopia can bypass POTRAZ data-controller
licensing entirely via a "zero-storage" architecture (ephemeral in-browser auth, no PII
database, on-chain-only action verification). **That argument does not hold for the main
Exotopia platform as it exists today** — the account system (Supabase-backed, storing email
and settlement/citizen-science data per `legal-privacy.md` § 2) makes us a data controller
under SI 155's definition regardless of the blockchain framing, and the on-chain-ledger
component the note describes was removed from the product on 2026-08-01 (see
`legal-terms.md` changelog). Two distinct deployments need separate analysis:

- **Main Exotopia platform (accounts, eco-ops submissions, settlement records):** ordinary
  data-controller analysis applies. Likely requires POTRAZ registration/licensing under SI
  155 if Zimbabwean residents create accounts or submit citizen-science data, notification to
  POTRAZ before any cross-border transfer to our (US-hosted) Supabase project under CDPA
  § 25, and verifiable parental consent for any under-18 participant per the CDPA's children's
  data provisions — relevant for school- or youth-group-based eco-ops drives specifically.
  24-hour breach notification to POTRAZ applies once we are a registered controller.
- **Static offline eco-library node** (`hub.approvideo.org`-style static media/video library,
  served from a local Raspberry Pi or Wi-Fi mesh box in a community center or school, no
  backend, no accounts, no data collection): this deployment genuinely does not process
  personal data and is the strongest candidate for falling outside SI 155's licensing trigger
  entirely — matches the "static, no external SaaS dependency" deployment path already
  described in `SPEC_SELF_HOSTED_NETWORK.md` § 3. This is the piece worth building first for
  Zimbabwe specifically, given mobile data costs (among the highest in SADC) and intermittent
  connectivity in areas like Epworth and Chitungwiza.

Key questions:
- Does an offline, zero-account static node avoid SI 155's data-controller licensing trigger
  entirely, or does POTRAZ's definition reach any entity "determining the purpose of
  collecting personal data" even without a backend (e.g. via device/usage analytics we'd need
  to explicitly not collect)?
- If Zimbabwean users are directed from the static node to the main Exotopia platform (to
  claim a settlement, join eco-ops program tracking, etc.), does that hand-off itself trigger
  CDPA obligations at the point of referral, or only once an account is actually created?
- What does "community verification via on-device, workshop-lead-signed QR codes" (the note's
  proposed pattern for anonymous youth-safe action rewards) actually require technically, and
  does it avoid children's-data consent requirements only if no name/age/biometric data is
  captured anywhere in the flow, including analytics?
- POTRAZ licensing fee schedule and registration timeline under SI 155 — not yet researched.

- Can eco-ops field data (soil, biodiversity, rainfall) collected in climate-stressed zones be made available to CGIAR, FAO, or IPCC researchers under an open data licence without compromising community data sovereignty?

## Cross-cutting issues

### Open-source and right of access

Our platform code is GPL v3. Anyone can run a compliant instance. The astronomical data is public domain (NASA, ESA). The address format is an open specification. We believe this open-access architecture provides strong protection in jurisdictions that attempt to restrict access — a legal entity can be restricted but cannot un-publish open-source code or delete public blockchain records.

### Privacy and data

We hold minimal PII. The main "personal data" in our system is:
- The blockchain address (wallet address) linked to a settlement — this is pseudonymous, not nominative
- Eco-ops field data (location, activity type) linked to a user account — governed by pon.ink's privacy policy, not ours
- Email addresses in the pon.ink account system — not stored by Exotopia.org directly

Our strategy: minimise data we hold to the point where GDPR, CCPA, and equivalent laws apply primarily to pon.ink (which handles accounts) not to Exotopia.org (which handles coordinates).

### Financial sanctions

We operate under US OFAC, EU, and UN sanctions. Settlements may not be issued to, or on behalf of, sanctioned individuals or entities. This is enforced at the pon.ink payment layer. The free browsing tier has no such restriction — we cannot and will not apply geographic restrictions to access to astronomical data.

### Consumer protection across all jurisdictions

Regardless of local legal requirements, we commit to:
- Clear disclosure of all fees before purchase
- Accurate description of what is and is not permanent
- No misleading statements about investment value or returns
- Accessible refund/dispute policy
- Plain-language terms in the user's language where feasible
