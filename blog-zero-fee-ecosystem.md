## The standard split: 99 / 0.75 / 0.25

The Resonance Split across all PON INK / Exotopia / Ecocity / Worldbridger transactions:

- **99%** → Creator / artist / field worker wallet
- **0.75%** → Community Hardware Fund (WATSAN infrastructure, mapping nodes, field equipment)
- **0.25%** → Platform Maintenance (network ops, security, hosting)

Most NFT platforms take 5–15%. We take 1% total — and 0.75% of that goes directly to the physical infrastructure that field communities depend on, not to shareholders. The 0.25% platform share is the honest acknowledgement that running infrastructure has real costs.

**Special mintings and airdrop events can use different parameters via additional contracts.** Custom splits are fully configurable — event partnerships, NGO-funded campaigns, supply chain group activities, consultancy projects — any split is valid as long as it's co-signed and logged on-chain. The 99/0.75/0.25 is the *default* for standard minting, not a cap.

## The honest answer to "what does the 0.75% Community Hardware Fund buy?"

Our actual sustainability mechanism: **$SUNLIGHT and ART token volume creates indirect value for the ecosystem — and 0.75% of every transaction funds the physical layer that makes that value real.**

What does this mean concretely?

When an artist mints $SUNLIGHT and it trades, the *existence of that transaction* — the fact that creative work is being recorded and exchanged — adds signal to the network. The 0.75% that flows to the Hardware Fund is accumulating toward the next WATSAN sensor node, the next mapping device, the next piece of field equipment that generates the eco-ops data that makes a settlement valuable.

When a field worker earns ART tokens for a water quality reading and lists them for resale, a real-world action now has a market expression. The platform took 0.25% of that — not 5%. The field worker kept 99%. And 0.75% is now building toward the next instrument in that field worker's toolkit.

The indirect value mechanism:
- Token volume → network legitimacy + Hardware Fund accumulation → more participants + better field tools
- More participants → more eco-ops data → richer mule-bot corpora
- Richer corpora → better settlement outcomes → higher-quality $SUNLIGHT recordings and ART token claims
- Higher quality → real-world recognition → grants, partnerships, NGO attention → operational sustainability for SCD Hub (the non-profit behind this)

The 0.25% platform share sustains infrastructure operations. The SCD Hub is additionally sustained by grant funding, partnership income, and the credibility that comes from running a network that demonstrably creates value for field communities without extracting from them.

## The token economy — what needs management

Three tokens, three different dynamics:

**$SUNLIGHT** — creative recording NFT. No fixed supply. Value comes from:
- The quality and authenticity of what's recorded (real artists, real field sounds)
- The licensing terms encoded on-chain
- Secondary market activity (99% to original creator on resale; 0.75% Hardware Fund; 0.25% platform)

**ART (Activity Reward Tokens)** — eco-ops rewards. Earned through verified field work. Value comes from:
- Scarcity relative to field activity (you can only earn one if you do the work)
- Sponsorship demand (organisations wanting to fund field work buy ART eligibility)
- Resalability — a field worker can sell ART to someone who wants to offset or sponsor without doing the work themselves

**Exolocation Deed** — virtual land NFT. No fixed supply globally (there are 5,000+ exoplanets with many 10°×10° plots each). Value comes from:
- Settlement activity (an active settlement with eco-ops history is worth more than an empty plot)
- Rarity (certain planets, certain coordinate systems, certain trophic levels)
- mule-bot corpus quality (a well-maintained mule-bot corpus makes the settlement more useful)

The thing that needs "management" is not supply — it's **velocity and quality**. 
- $SUNLIGHT: too much low-quality = the token means nothing. Need curation / social proof.
- ART: too many unverified claims = the token loses its meaning as proof-of-work. Need the eco-ops verification chain to stay tamper-evident.
- Exolocation: too many empty plots = settlement activity is the differentiator. mule-bot becomes the signal of settlement quality.

## What "self-optimizing" means for programmers and engineers

This is worth unpacking precisely because we're using it as a marketing claim that needs to be technically grounded.

**Self-optimizing does NOT mean:**
- The platform automatically changes its own code
- An AI makes economic decisions autonomously
- The system improves without human input

**Self-optimizing DOES mean:**
- The incentive structure is designed so that individually-rational actions aggregate to collective benefit
- mule-bot's corpus improves as the settlement owner does more eco-ops (their actions automatically enrich their own tool)
- The Ecommunity DAO governance means parameter changes (what types of eco-ops get rewarded, how ART tokens are weighted) are made by participants who are economically aligned with good outcomes
- Token volume is self-reinforcing: active settlements attract more $SUNLIGHT recordings → more Hub Approvideo content → richer mule-bot corpora → better settlement outcomes → more eco-ops activity → more ART volume

As engineers: the self-optimization is a **feedback loop design problem**. We've built the data structures (eco-ops on-chain, mule-bot local corpus, $SUNLIGHT licensing, ART resalability) such that the components reinforce each other when participants behave in alignment with the mission.

The platform doesn't need to extract a fee because the value that participants create by participating flows back into the ecosystem's health. This is the actual mechanism.

## mule-bot's role in the economic model

mule-bot is not just a feature. It's the **quality signal** for the settlement economy.

A settlement with a well-maintained mule-bot corpus is demonstrably more active:
- Its eco-ops check-in history is visible in the hashmark
- Its Hub Approvideo curation is up to date
- Its $SUNLIGHT earnings tracker is connected to real performance
- Its participation planning is working — the settlement is earning rewards

This makes the settlement's Exolocation Deed worth more on the secondary market. Which means the original minter (who earned 99% of the resale) gets nearly all of the value for their activity — with 0.75% going to the field hardware that made the eco-ops data possible.

mule-bot's definition — "land-connected, replicable, programmable electronic mule" — is economic as much as technical:
- **Land-connected**: its value is grounded in real-world activity (eco-ops, water quality, soil health)
- **Replicable**: the same mule-bot can be deployed to any settlement; its knowledge is transferable
- **Programmable**: the settlement owner can configure it, extend its corpus, point it at new data
- **Electronic mule**: it does the heavy lifting — tracking, planning, curating — so the human can focus on the actual field work

## Questions still open (for us to discuss)

1. **ART token resale market** — answered. Buyers are:
   - **Event-driven promoters** — brands, venues, community orgs buying ART to airdrop at events
   - **Art partners** — galleries and cultural producers buying ART as participation signal in the regenerative network
   - **Gift purchasers** — people who want to recognise field workers without doing the work themselves, buying ART to airdrop
   - **Early-distribution recipients** — ART goes out free initially to grow a targeted audience; rarity and utility create secondary demand after the initial free distribution
   - **Supply chain groups** — e.g. a shredded-plastic collective earns ART from a cleanup, some members resell to fund the next cleanup
   
   The mechanism: ART is **proof-of-work NFT with community-defined supply** — not speculation, but **recognition liquidity**. Someone who can't do the physical work can express solidarity by buying ART from someone who did.

2. **$SUNLIGHT curation**: how do we prevent low-quality recordings from diluting the token? Is this a community governance question or a technical filter?

3. **mule-bot corpus quality**: what makes one corpus better than another? Is it size, recency, verification status of the eco-ops data? We should define quality metrics.

4. **The DAO treasury**: the 0.25% platform share flows to operations. If development needs exceed this, what's the path? Community vote to adjust the split? If so, what's the cap and who votes?

5. **Communicating 0.25% clearly**: we want people to understand that 0.75% goes to field hardware (not "to the platform") and 0.25% goes to platform ops (not "to shareholders"). How do we explain this without making it sound like a hidden fee when it's actually the smallest possible responsible share?
